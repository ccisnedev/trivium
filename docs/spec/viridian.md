# Spec — Ensō, Viridian and the Tree of Noesis

> Status: draft
> Version: 0.4.0
> Purpose: define the base cosmology, entry scene and central pedagogical metaphor of Trivium.

---

## Summary

Knowel Adventure does not start with a dashboard screen. It starts with a rite.

The app opens on a black screen. The user traces an **Ensō** — a zen circle. That gesture is the ritual of entry. Upon completing it, the user arrives at a dark coast facing the sea. It is night. On the horizon shines a distant green light: **Viridian**.

From Viridian's direction come petals carried by the tide. Those petals come from the **Tree of Noesis**, a cosmic structure of knowledge whose function is not to forbid it, but to organize it. Each petal represents a learning possibility: a question, a route, a knowel not yet integrated.

The world's premise is simple: in this place, the entropy of knowledge runs in reverse. What in the normal world appears as noise, dispersion and indeterminacy, here can become form, path and skill through inquiry.

---

## Spec Objective

This spec defines:

- the product's cosmological language
- the initial onboarding scene
- the symbolic function of Ensō, Viridian, the Tree of Noesis and petals
- the translation of that cosmology into pedagogical and game mechanics
- the semantic and visual boundaries of this direction

This spec does not yet define:

- detailed wireframes
- technical architecture for rendering or input
- economy, pricing or monetization system
- complete system of regions, factions or expanded lore

## Base Cosmology

### Canonical Ontology

- **Trivium**: the digital world. The crossing of three roads: learning, work and virtual life. See [world.md](world.md) for the complete spec.
- **Viridian**: the green light on the horizon. Its function is to give direction. It must always feel more like promise and orientation than a finally possessed destination.
- **Tree of Noesis**: the structure of knowledge. Has a sakura presence, not as a literal Japanese reference, but as a species native to the universe.
- **Petals**: knowel possibilities. Latent forms of learning before being integrated by the user.
- **Knowels**: verified capabilities. Not pieces of content viewed, but skills the user can already demonstrate.

### Ensō

**Ensō** (円相) is the zen circle — a single brushstroke that represents wholeness, the void and the moment of creation. In Trivium, the Ensō is the ritual gesture of entry. It is not a menu, a button or a paywall screen. It is an act of crossing.

Conceptual function:

- separates the world of noise from the world of clarity
- turns entering the product into a ritual decision, not a utilitarian interaction
- reinforces that accessing the full experience implies moving from indeterminacy to order
- the imperfection of the hand-drawn circle is intentional: it signals that the journey begins from wherever you are

Product function:

- serves as the entry gesture into the world
- can be used as a state transition between free exploration and structured map
- can become a persistent metaphor for subscription, re-entry and continuity

### Viridian

**Viridian** is the green light on the horizon. It is not a star or a lamp. It may be associated with a distant planet, but in the user's experience it functions primarily as direction, call and promise.

Conceptual function:

- represents the horizon of learning
- embodies the promise of clarity, form and structure not yet conquered
- orients the journey without exhausting itself as a closed final goal

Emotional function:

- must feel like Gatsby's green light: call, promise, desire to arrive
- does not communicate instant reward, but sustained orientation and longing
- must remain partially unreachable to preserve its symbolic force

### The World

**Trivium** is the digital world where inquiry, work and virtual life take place. It opens upon tracing the Ensō and contains the real experience of learning and collaboration.

Conceptual function:

- is the territory where a problematic situation can take form
- turns learning into navigation, exploration and articulation
- gives body to the idea that knowing is orienting oneself better within a living structure
- materializes internet metaphors as navigable spaces

Relationship with Viridian:

- Viridian does not replace Trivium; it orients it
- the user inhabits Trivium, not the light itself
- Viridian's light gives direction to the experience without becoming a consumable ending

See [world.md](world.md) for the complete world spec.

### The Tree of Noesis

**The Tree of Noesis** is the cosmic structure of knowledge. It must not be based on the biblical figure of the tree of knowledge.

Decision:

- the biblical referent is discarded as the primary symbol

Rationale:

- introduces associations of guilt, fall and prohibition
- displaces the knowledge metaphor toward transgression
- does not match the product's pedagogical vision

Instead, the Tree of Noesis will be:

- a species native to the Trivium universe
- visually inspired by the sakura's delicacy
- conceptually tied to inquiry, formation and the emergence of meaning

Conceptual function:

- structures knowledge as living, branching relationships
- organizes knowledge as living growth
- turns understanding into blooming, not dead accumulation
- expresses that learning is forming relationships, not collecting data

Initial visual direction:

- silhouette close to sakura
- light petals with green or pearlescent reflections
- branches with organic geometry suggesting circular patterns and intelligent branching
- sense of living fragility, not rigid monument

### Petals

**Petals** are knowel possibilities. They are seeds of future integration.

Conceptual function:

- each petal represents a latent form of knowledge
- it is not mastery yet; it is a possibility of inquiry
- the user does not consume petals, they incorporate them

Product function:

- can represent invitations to routes or concepts
- can be used as artifacts of discovery, memory or unlocking
- can become the minimal visual language of knowels not yet integrated

## Philosophical Foundation

The cosmology must be compatible with a pragmatist intuition:

- learning starts in an indeterminate situation
- inquiry transforms that situation into something clearer, more articulate and more operable
- knowledge is valuable because it allows orienting, acting and continuing to learn

In product terms, this implies:

- the user does not enter to consume information, but to resolve indeterminacy
- each knowel must feel like a conquered skill, not content viewed
- progress is not measured only by quantity, but by the ability to form paths, connections and criteria

The world's fantasy must not hide this thesis. It must make it tangible.

## Onboarding Scene

### Base Sequence

1. Completely black screen.
2. No visible UI except a minimal invitation to trace an Ensō.
3. The user draws the circle.
4. The Ensō completes — the gesture opens the way.
5. The darkness opens toward Trivium's dark coast.
6. The user appears facing the sea.
7. Viridian's green glow is visible on the horizon.
8. The tide brings petals from the Tree of Noesis.
9. The world suggests that each petal is a path possibility.
10. From there, guided exploration begins.

### Scene Objectives

- communicate that entering requires intention
- establish the universe's tone in under 30 seconds
- replace an explanatory onboarding with a memorable experience
- present learning as a journey, not a catalog

### Constraints

- do not over-explain lore in the first session
- do not fill the screen with expository text
- do not break the atmosphere with conventional UI too early
- do not turn the Ensō into a gimmick without semantic consequences

## Translation to Product Mechanics

### Knowels

Knowels are the user's verified capabilities. In the world's language, they can emerge from petals and take root in the user's personal tree within Trivium.

This allows the system to communicate:

- discovery when a knowel appears as a possibility
- integration when the user understands and demonstrates it
- growth when that knowel unlocks new branches

### Personal Tree

The user maintains a personal Wisdom Tree. The Tree of Noesis is the cosmic tree; the user's tree is its cultivated reflection.

Relationship between both:

- the Tree of Noesis contains the possible forms of knowledge
- the personal tree shows which forms have already been incorporated by that user

### Progress

Progress must feel like an increase in capability, not simple point accumulation.

Recommended direction:

- use skills, branches, routes and mastery as the primary language
- if points exist, they should be a secondary abstraction of growth
- favor visualizations of connections and unlocks over bare counters

### Review and Forgetting

The petal and blooming metaphor allows expressing memory and forgetting without school-style punishment.

Examples:

- a petal can lose brightness if a skill is not revisited
- a branch can request care, not just mechanical repetition
- review can feel like watering or reviving a blooming

## Visual Language

### Initial Palette

- deep black as the state of indeterminacy and silence
- viridian green as a signal of orientation, promise and emergent order
- soft whites or pearlescents for petals, mist and interface accents

### Materiality

- nocturnal sea with minimal reflections
- distant, clean green light, never saturated like aggressive neon
- particles and petals with slow, directional movement

### Tone

- contemplative, precise, ritual
- fantastic without falling into generic medieval fantasy
- more pilgrimage and inquiry than literal combat and conquest

---

## Narrative Language

Guiding phrases for the system:

- Trace the Ensō.
- Viridian awaits you.
- A petal has reached the shore.
- This possibility has not yet taken root.
- Your tree has gained a new branch.
- The uncertain begins to take form.

Editorial principle:

- the system must speak as the world's guide, not as a corporate dashboard
- the tone must be sober and evocative, never overwrought or pseudo-mystical without precision

## Decisions Made

- Ensō is the ritual gesture of entry — the zen circle traced to cross into Trivium.
- Trivium is the world's name: the crossing of learning, work and virtual life.
- Viridian is the green light on the horizon: direction and promise, never a fully exhaustible goal.
- The Tree of Noesis is the cosmic structure of knowledge.
- Petals are knowel possibilities and seeds of integration.
- Knowels are verified capabilities.
- The biblical reference to the tree of knowledge will not be used as the central basis.
- The tree's visual reference will be a sakura reinterpreted as a species native to the universe.

---

## Open Questions

- How Viridian's presence is represented in future phases without breaking its condition as a never fully reachable horizon.
- Whether the Ensō should be mandatory on every entry or reserved for first time and special moments.
- Whether petals should map to individual knowels, routes or both depending on context.
- Whether the Tree of Noesis should be shown explicitly from the start or revealed gradually.
- Whether skill point language remains visible to the user or gets absorbed by branches, mastery and tree.

---

## Success Criterion for This Direction

The direction works if a user can describe the initial experience like this:

> I entered by tracing an Ensō. I crossed into Trivium. I saw a green planet in the distance. I felt that learning here was not about viewing content, but entering a world where questions become skills and paths.

If the experience is perceived as just an aesthetic layer on a course app, this direction fails.
